Trenchline Privacy Policy

Effective date: 14 November 2026

Trenchline ("the game") is a real-time territory control strategy game published by Burak Seker ("we"). This policy explains what the game does with data. It is short, because the game itself does very little with data: it is played on your device against computer opponents, it has no accounts, and it asks you for nothing. What goes to the internet is the advertising and the usage statistics described below.

Internet connection

The game is free because of its ads, so a battle needs an internet connection: when the device has no connection, the battle pauses and continues where it left off once the connection is back. Menus, settings and past games work without one.

What we collect

We have no server of our own. We use two Google services, Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Remote Config:

  • Google Analytics for Firebase gives us anonymous usage statistics: a random ID for the installation (not your name or account), screen views and sessions, the ads shown, device model, operating system, app version, language and approximate location (country or region) derived from the IP address. We use them to see how the game is played and how many ads are shown, never to identify you. It follows your ad consent choice: where you decline, it does not collect.
  • Firebase Remote Config lets the game check whether a newer version is required. To do that it sends the installation ID, app and operating system version, country and language.

How Google handles this data: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

What stays on your device

The game keeps the following in the device's private app storage and sends none of it to us:

  • your saved battles: the state and result of your last 30 battles (Past Games);
  • your settings: the difficulty you chose, and whether sound, autosave, the minimap and high resolution are on.

If Android's own backup is turned on, Android may back these files up to your Google account; that backup is yours and we have no access to it.

Deleting them is up to you: Ayarlar → Tüm kayıtları sil (Settings → Delete all saves) in the game removes the saved battles, Android Settings → Apps → Trenchline → Storage → Clear data resets everything, and uninstalling the game leaves nothing behind.

Advertising

The game shows a full-screen ad as a battle starts (at most one every 3 minutes). The ads are served through Unity LevelPlay, an ad mediation service of Unity Technologies, by its ad networks (Unity Ads and ironSource Ads). To do so, the advertising software inside the game collects and processes device data: your device's advertising ID and other device identifiers, IP address, approximate location derived from it, device and operating system information, diagnostics, and your interactions with ads.

That processing is Unity's, not ours; we only see aggregate counts of how many ads were shown and what they earned. How Unity uses this data is described here:

  • Unity Privacy Policy: https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time under Android Settings → Privacy → Ads.

Consent: if you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland

Before requesting a single ad, the game shows you a consent form through Google's User Messaging Platform, a certified consent management platform. No ad is requested until you answer, and your answer decides whether the ads you see are personalised and whether usage statistics are collected.

You can change your answer at any time: Ayarlar → Reklam gizlilik seçenekleri (Settings → Ad privacy options) in the game opens the form again. That option appears only where the form is shown.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

Permissions

The game asks for internet access and network state (so that ads can be fetched and a battle can pause when the connection drops), the advertising ID and Android's ad services permissions, along with the wake lock and foreground service permissions that Google's libraries use to finish background work. The game does not ask for the camera, microphone, location, contacts, files or storage.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published at this address and the effective date above changes with it.
